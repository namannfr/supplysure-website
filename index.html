Supplier readiness for UK construction supply chains

From approved to ready.

SupplySure turns supplier evidence into opportunity-specific, explainable readiness decisions, so buyers know which suppliers are ready for the job, and suppliers know what they need to fix before they bid.

The problem

Approval is not the same as readiness.

UK construction runs on subcontracted packages. Every supplier must prove it is insured, competent, solvent, tax-compliant and safe. Buyers and suppliers can prove approval. Neither can easily prove readiness for a specific opportunity.

Static approval

An accreditation certifies a general standard at a point in time. It does not say whether a supplier meets the requirements of this package, on this project, on this date.

Evidence duplication

Evidence sits across accreditation portals, ERP supplier records, shared drives, spreadsheets and email. Each new project chases and checks it again.

Late discovery

Buyers and suppliers often find a missing requirement only after bid or mobilisation effort has been spent. Typical SME bid preparation costs £500 to £4,000 per tender.

Bid cost figure: CostEstimator UK, Q4 2025 base, as cited in the SupplySure concept note.

What SupplySure does

A readiness layer for complex supply chains.

SupplySure ingests evidence suppliers already hold, keeps it live against expiry, maps it against structured project requirements, and returns an explainable readiness decision. It sits above accreditation schemes and procurement systems. It does not replace them.

  1. 01

    Ingest

    Certificates, insurance, accounts, ISO evidence, competence records, environmental evidence and CDP share-code data the supplier already holds.

  2. 02

    Structure

    Evidence and requirements are converted into machine-readable data.

  3. 03

    Map

    Supplier evidence is matched to the requirements of a specific project, package or tender.

  4. 04

    Decide

    A deterministic rules engine produces the readiness result.

  5. 05

    Explain

    Every result links back to the source evidence behind it.

  6. 06

    Act

    Corrective actions and expiry workflows tell users exactly what to fix.

Explainability

Compliance decisions you can explain.

SupplySure does not use a black-box model to decide readiness. The same evidence, requirement set and date always produce the same result, so a decision can be defended in an audit.

AI proposes.

Extraction with citations

Rules decide.

Deterministic and reproducible

Humans confirm.

Review of uncertain cases

  • AI extracts information from documents. It does not issue the verdict.
  • Each extracted field is tied to a citation in the source document.
  • An independent verification step checks the extracted information.
  • A deterministic rules engine evaluates the requirements.
  • People review the uncertain cases.
  • Audit records preserve provenance.

Innovation

The innovation is not “AI-powered compliance.”

Document extraction is a commodity capability. The change SupplySure is built around is the move from standard-level approval to opportunity-specific, date-aware readiness, and the data model that makes it reusable.

From

Standard-level approval

“Is this supplier approved?”

↓→

To

Opportunity-specific, date-aware readiness

“Is this supplier ready for this opportunity, on this date, and what is missing?”

I-1

Opportunity-specific readiness

MVP
What exists today
Standard-level approval from schemes and registers, typically valid for a year.
What SupplySure changes
Readiness is evaluated against a specific requirement set, at the moment of the opportunity, using live evidence.
Why it matters
The question buyers face at mobilisation is about this package and this date, and a general approval does not answer it.
How it can be measured
Late-stage compliance failures per 100 mobilisations, before and after.

I-2

Rules-as-data requirement library

MVP
What exists today
Requirements live in tender documents, emails and reviewers’ heads.
What SupplySure changes
Versioned templates by sector, buyer and project archetype. A buyer configures once and applies many times.
Why it matters
A buyer’s template library becomes a reusable asset, and a new sector needs new templates, not a new product.
How it can be measured
Template reuse rate and requirement-set build time.

I-3

Explainable evidence intelligence

MVP
What exists today
Manual reading, or extraction with no provenance.
What SupplySure changes
Every field, verdict and action carries source-document provenance. Corrective actions are generated from the failing requirement.
Why it matters
The output has to survive an audit.
How it can be measured
Classification accuracy after human review, and audit findings traced to source.

I-4

Two-sided readiness feedback

Planned · from v2
What exists today
Suppliers learn of gaps at rejection.
What SupplySure changes
Suppliers see the gap list for a live opportunity before they bid.
Why it matters
Bid effort is spent only where the compliance gate can be cleared.
How it can be measured
Gaps identified before submission, and wasted bid cost avoided.

I-5

Compounding readiness corpus

Future · long term
What exists today
No system learns which evidence patterns precede mobilisation problems.
What SupplySure changes
Requirement templates, evidence and outcome data improve readiness precision over time.
Why it matters
It exists only once the first four pillars generate data, which makes it hard to copy.
How it can be measured
Readiness precision, verdict against mobilisation outcome, tracked quarterly.

Why SupplySure is different

Complementary to the systems you already use.

Each of these does its own job well. SupplySure consumes the evidence they produce and answers the one question they leave open: is this supplier ready for this opportunity?

Existing system What it solves What remains unsolved How SupplySure fits
Accreditation schemes and registers Pre-qualification against a common standard, with periodic assessment. Point-in-time, standard-level approval. No project-specific requirement mapping and no cross-scheme evidence view. Consumes their certificates as evidence
Central Digital Platform Core supplier information stored once and shared by code with public contracting authorities. Public contracting authorities and core information only. Tender-specific questionnaires remain. Private supply chains are outside scope. Planned import of share-code data
Procurement suites Supplier records, onboarding workflow, spend and contract data. Built around the buyer’s process. Requirement logic is generic and there is no supplier-facing gap view. Sits alongside as the readiness layer
Construction ERP systems Supplier master, commercial and cost management. Systems of record, not readiness engines. Planned CSV / API exchange
Generic TPRM / GRC platforms Cyber, ESG and financial risk monitoring for large enterprises. A different buyer and different evidence. Not built around project requirements. Different question, compatible
AI bid-writing tools Draft tender responses. They write the bid. They do not verify readiness. Readiness check before the bid is written
Spreadsheets and consultants Flexible, familiar and cheap to start. Unaudited, not reusable and blind to expiry. One-off checks do not scale. Replaces the manual chase with a reusable record

SupplySure is not an accreditation scheme, a certification body or a tender adjudication system. It does not replace the Common Assessment Standard, SSIP schemes or the Central Digital Platform.

Why the UK, why now?

A mature assurance ecosystem, with one gap left open.

The UK has standardised how suppliers are approved. What remains is the gap between approval and opportunity-specific readiness.

  1. 2022

    Building Safety Act 2022

    Duty-holders are expected to verify supply-chain competence, not rely on declarations.

  2. Apr 2023

    PAS 91 withdrawn

    BSI withdraws the earlier pre-qualification standard.

  3. 2023

    Procurement Act 2023

    New public procurement regime passed into law.

  4. 2024

    PPN 03/24

    Confirms the Common Assessment Standard for public construction works.

  5. 24 Feb 2025

    Central Digital Platform live

    The Procurement Act commences. Suppliers store core information once and share it by code.

  • CAS and SSIP ecosystem

    Buyers and suppliers already accept reusable evidence, yet suppliers still sit across multiple schemes.

  • More evidence, more specific

    Each reform increases the number and specificity of requirements, and how sensitive they are to expiry.

  • SME procurement pressure

    Public buyers are measured on SME inclusion. SMEs need to show readiness to take part.

  • A subcontracted ecosystem

    Tier 1 delivery runs on subcontracted packages, so the readiness question is asked at scale.

SupplySure is a commercial product. It is not a regulator, and using it is not a legal requirement under any of the legislation above.

Scalability

Built to scale through data, templates and network effects.

  • Reusable passport network

    A supplier’s passport serves every buyer on the network. Each new buyer makes the passport more useful to the supplier, and each new supplier makes the platform more useful to buyers.

  • Rules-as-data expansion

    Entering a new sector mainly requires a new template library, not a new product. FM, housing and infrastructure share the same evidence types with different requirement weightings.

  • Operating leverage

    Buyer onboarding becomes template-led over time, and supplier onboarding is designed to be self-serve.

These are design intentions. The network effect depends on buyer and supplier adoption that has not yet happened.
